How to use this template
  1. Fill in the taxpayer details below — these print on the sheet.
  2. Work down the Assets column, then the Liabilities & Owner’s Funds column. Leave anything you don’t have as blank — blank is treated as zero.
  3. Check the balance check panel that appears under the sheet. Total assets must equal total liabilities plus owner’s funds. A difference means something is missing or mis-stated — the panel lists the usual causes.
  4. Use the Income Statement tab for your turnover and costs. Enter each expense as a rate and the period it covers — the sheet annualises it. The net profit after tax and the tax charge flow straight into the balance sheet above, so the two statements always agree.
  5. Use the Assets Register tab for the itemised fixed-asset list URA asks you to keep, then print or export.

Important: this is a working paper, not the URA return form. The official DT-2001 Excel template must still be downloaded from the URA portal, completed, validated and uploaded. Transfer your figures from here into that template. Nothing is sent anywhere — your figures stay in this browser.

What this form is for

Uganda’s Income Tax Act, section 118(5) requires that a return of income by a taxpayer carrying on business shall be accompanied by a statement of income and expenditure and a statement of the assets and liabilities of the taxpayer. For an individual trading as a sole proprietor that statement is filed on form DT-2001 (Income Tax Return for Individual with Business Income). This template is a working paper for both statements. The Balance Sheet tab uses the same line-item taxonomy as URA’s own balance sheet (Section E of the income tax return), calculates the subtotals for you, and tells you straight away whether the statement balances. The Income Statement tab builds the statement of income and expenditure, works out the tax, and feeds the net profit and the tax provision straight into the balance sheet so the two always agree.

It is not the return. You still have to download the official DT-2001 Excel template from the URA portal, type your figures in, validate and upload. Use this sheet to work out your numbers and as the supporting statement that accompanies the return.

Using the Income Statement tab

The Income Statement tab is a working paper for the statement of income and expenditure that the Income Tax Act, section 118(5) requires you to file alongside the return. It works like this:

  1. Turnover. Enter your total sales for the year of income. If you sell goods, add what you bought and deduct closing stock to get the cost of sales and your gross profit. If you run a service business with no trading stock, leave the cost of sales lines empty.
  2. Other income. Interest, rent, and anything that is not trading income goes here, so it is not swept into turnover.
  3. Operating expenses. Enter the rate you pay and choose the period it covers — per day, per month or per year. The sheet multiplies it out for you (per day × 365, per month × 12, per year × 1) and shows the equivalent monthly cost next to it. Compare that monthly column with what you actually pay each month; if it does not match, the period is wrong. Ordinary repairs and maintenance are deductible. Repairs that improve or extend an asset are capital and belong in fixed assets instead.
  4. Profit. Profit before tax is turnover plus other income, less cost of sales, less operating expenses. Add back any depreciation you have charged (it is not deductible as an expense) and deduct capital allowances actually claimed, to arrive at chargeable income.
  5. Tax. The tax is worked out on the band table below. The net profit after tax flows automatically to the Balance Sheet tab, into owner’s funds (line E9b) and into the provision for income tax (line E3diia) — so the two tabs can never disagree.
  6. Provisional tax instalments. Four instalments of 25% of the estimated annual tax are shown with their due dates, less anything you have already paid.
Estimates are fine for a provisional return. A provisional return is based on your best estimate of the year of income. You may file an amended provisional return at any time before the year of income ends if your figures change, and the final return replaces the estimate.

If the statement does not balance

The check panel under the sheet shows the difference. In practice it is nearly always one of these:

  • Profit for the year is missing or wrong. Net profit must come from your income statement, after tax, and must agree with it exactly.
  • Drawings are understated — money taken out for personal use is a drawing, not an expense, and reduces capital rather than profit.
  • Capital introduced is missing — money or assets brought in during the year increases capital but is not income.
  • A liability is missing — unpaid VAT, PAYE, NSSF, a tax provision, a loan instalment, or an amount owing to URA (the “Balance with URA” line).
  • An asset is missing — stock counted but not valued, or a fixed asset bought on credit that is also unrecorded in creditors.
  • Accumulated depreciation is out of step with the capital allowance claimed in the income statement.
  • Goods on hire purchase are sometimes only half recognised — the asset and the outstanding hire purchase liability both need to appear.

Helpful ratios

URA officers commonly look at these when reviewing a return:

  • Current ratio = total current assets ÷ total current liabilities and provisions. Below 1.0 means short-term obligations exceed short-term resources — expect a question.
  • Gearing = loan funds plus deferred tax liability ÷ owner’s funds. High gearing next to a thin profit is worth explaining.
  • Net current assets should be positive for a going concern.

Record keeping

Keep for five years after the end of the tax period: sales invoices and EFRIS e-invoices/e-receipts, purchase invoices, bank and mobile money statements, payroll records, the assets register, the stock records, and the filed return with any URA assessment.
