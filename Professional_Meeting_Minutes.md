## 2.0 Communication from the Executive Director

### 2.1 SACCO development and organisational coordination

The ED reported on her meeting with Salim Saleh and explained that she had presented a concept aimed at improving the SACCO.

She emphasised equality within the organisation and explained the process for borrowing money from the SACCO, noting that the process could strengthen coordination among members.

She raised a concern about the district’s engagement in Busika and discussed efforts to access Parish Development Model (PDM) funding for women. The specific district concern was not elaborated in the notes.

### 2.2 Wine production, GROW and enterprise expansion

She discussed banana wine production and processing, as well as expanding activities referred to in the notes as “CUF” in different areas to improve living standards. The acronym has been retained as written and requires confirmation.

She reported that the organisation had secured a GROW project and that engineers had visited to prepare bills of quantities.

She discussed the need for donor support for pineapple wine production and separate machinery, noting that banana and pineapple production could not be combined.

She encouraged women to expand from the mother branch and replicate similar projects in their villages.

She indicated that participants were expected to begin earning income from wine production in December. The year was not specified.

### 2.3 Marketing, digital support and financial skills

The ED called for training in marketing, supported by digital devices that would help members market the wine they produce. She appealed for resource persons to assist with the training.

She also emphasised the need to teach members accounting skills.

## 5.0 Cooperative Training — Jonathan Sselubiri, UCA

### 5.1 Role and structure of the cooperative movement

The UCA representative described the Alliance as a national umbrella body that brings SACCOs and cooperatives together. He outlined the primary, secondary, tertiary and apex levels, identifying the apex as the overall umbrella level.

### 5.2 Registration, probation and compliance

He explained that a group passes through a preparation/probation stage and is monitored for compliance before receiving a final certificate.

He noted that certification may be withdrawn if the organisation fails to follow cooperative requirements, including in cases involving misuse of authority. Cooperative rules were to be applied carefully and members were expected to comply with the law.

### 5.3 Leadership and governance

Members were advised to choose effective leaders who could mobilise others and to review the group’s progress from its establishment to its current position. The SACCO was described as a business requiring committed members and serious leadership.

Leadership training, elections and changes in leadership were discussed. A four-year leadership term was mentioned; this should be checked against the SACCO’s approved bylaws.

The group was advised to involve or consult the DCO on leadership and registration matters. Where the DCO is unavailable, a representative may be sent. Registration documents and resolutions were to be discussed with the DCO before submission to the registrar.

### 5.4 Membership, shares and registration requirements

The requirements listed included national identification (NID) for every member, voluntary participation, compliance with the law, business capital, and payment of membership fees and omugabo (shares).

Membership money was described as supporting group mobilisation, while shares were identified as business capital and not funds for salaries or unrelated expenditure.

The notes state that registration should be supported by a resolution and refer to more than 30 members, bylaws/regulation books, four copies, a UGX 20,000 subscription/share amount connected with the head office, and payment of UGX 60,000 to the ministry.

The exact basis of the UGX 20,000 charge and the applicability of the other figures are not fully clear. All requirements, amounts and copy numbers should be confirmed with the DCO/UCA before payment or submission.

### 5.5 Member services, finance and GROW

The session also covered member qualification, enterprise education for women, microfinance, market access, and coordination with the ministry regarding export licensing.

The notes link an association certificate with improved access to finance and record an interest rate of 6% per annum. The particular loan or financial product to which this rate applies is not identified.

The GROW discussion emphasised women organising under cooperatives to access government support. Taxation of individuals in different sectors was also mentioned.
