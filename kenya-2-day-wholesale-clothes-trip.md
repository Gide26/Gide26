# Two Days in Kenya — Wholesale Clothing Buying Trip

**Traveller:** solo · **Base:** Nairobi (Kenya's wholesale garment hub) · **Hotel budget:** $15–20/night · **Purpose:** buy wholesale clothes
**Assumed start point:** Kampala (change this if you're flying in from elsewhere).

---

## 1. The plan at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Base city** | Nairobi (CBD / Ngara side — walkable-ish to the market) |
| **Main market** | **Gikomba** — East Africa's largest second-hand ("mitumba") clothing market, ~65,000 traders |
| **Second market** | **Eastleigh** — wholesale *new* clothes (jeans, dresses, suits, kids' wear, abayas) |
| **Hotel** | Guesthouse/hotel in Ngara or eastern CBD, ~$15–20/night, ~10–15 min from Gikomba |
| **Travel mode that makes 2 days work** | Fly Entebbe→Nairobi (~1h20). Bus is 13–16h each way — see §3 for how to still make it work |
| **Best shopping days** | New bales opened Mon–Thu; **Tuesday & Friday** are best for fresh stock. Arrive **5:30–7:00am** |
| **Money** | KES cash + **M-Pesa** (get a Safaricom SIM). USD accepted at forex bureaus only |

---

## 2. Know which market you actually want

"Wholesale clothes" in Nairobi means two totally different trades. Decide before you land:

| Market | What it sells | Price level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gikomba** (Muthurwa/Kamukunji area, east of CBD) | Second-hand (mitumba) bales & sorted lots — ladies' tops, dresses, jeans, men's shirts, kids' wear | Cheapest per piece | Sold by the **bale** (45–55kg standard) or by lots of 10–20 pcs. Prices jumped 40–60% since 2023 |
| **Eastleigh** (4th–6th Street, malls: Al Arafat, Amal, Tansim, Olympic, Pacific, New Mandara) | **New** clothes, wholesale & retail: dresses from ~KSh 200–350, sundresses wholesale from ~KSh 900, jeans, suits, khaki trousers, kids' wear, innerwear | Mid | Open later than Gikomba, mall-based, less chaotic, mostly cash/M-Pesa |
| **Kamukunji** (adjacent to Gikomba) | Shoes & some garments | Cheap | Combine with Gikomba in one trip |
| **Alpha Centre / Mombasa Road warehouses** | Importer-direct bales (before they reach Gikomba middlemen) | Wholesale | Worth it only if you're buying several bales |
| **Biashara Street / Luthuli Ave / OTC** | Fabric, khanga, kitenge, tailoring | Varies | Add-on only |

**If you want the cheapest stock → Gikomba. If you want clean, new, resellable boutique stock → Eastleigh.**

---

## 3. Getting there — and making 2 days actually work

| Mode | Time | Cost (one way) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flight** EBB→NBO | ~1h20 + airport time | from ~$120 one-way (Jambojet / Kenya Airways / Uganda Airlines / RwandAir — fares move a lot, book early) | ✅ Recommended. Only way to get a full 2 days of buying |
| **Bus** Kampala→Nairobi (Easy Coach, Modern Coast, Kampala Coach) | 13–16h | ~$19–35 | Cheap but eats ~3 days of your life round-trip. See pattern below |

**Recommended travel pattern (fly):**
- **Evening before Day 1:** late flight into Nairobi → check in → sleep. *(So you're at the market at dawn.)*
- **Day 1:** full buying day (Gikomba).
- **Day 2:** second market + freight your stock, then evening flight home.

**If you must take the bus (still fits "2 days in Kenya"):**
- Leave Kampala **night bus, Day 0** (e.g. 8pm) → arrive Nairobi ~11am–12pm Day 1.
- Day 1 afternoon: Eastleigh or Gikomba's afternoon/remnant trade (**"fagilia"** leftovers are sold off cheap at end of day).
- Day 2: Gikomba at 6am sharp → freight stock by 3pm → **night bus back Day 2** → home Day 3 morning.
- That is genuinely tight. Half a day of buying is all the bus option gives you.

**At JKIA:** airport is ~18km from CBD. Uber/Bolt ≈ KSh 1,500–2,500 (~$12–20); taxis KSh 2,500–3,500. Agree the fare or use the app — don't take random "taxi" touts inside the terminal.

## 4. Where to sleep on $15–20/night

**Best area for your purpose: Ngara / eastern CBD** — close to Gikomba, close to the CBD, and safer at night than the River Road strip, which is where the very cheapest rooms are.

Indicative single/double rates (these fluctuate by season and platform — always confirm with the hotel directly and ask for the **single room** rate; walk-in is often cheaper than booking sites):

| Hotel | Area | Indicative rate | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Khweza Bed & Breakfast** | Ngara Rd | ~$22–28 | The best of the "average hotel" bracket: rooftop restaurant, 17 en-suite rooms, ~5 min drive to CBD, close to Gikomba |
| **City Space Hotel** | Tom Mboya St, CBD | ~$12–15 | Cheap, central, walkable to transport |
| **Hotel Seldom** | CBD | ~$19 | Clean, decent reviews |
| **Aden Bay City Hotel** | CBD (near National Archives) | ~$20 | En-suite, restaurant, on budget |
| **Prearea / New Swanga** | CBD | ~$17–18 | Basic but within budget |
| **Deka Hotel Ngara** | Ngara | ~$12 | Cheapest sensible option; basic |
| **Sislink Babs Centre Hotel** | CBD | ~$11–13 | Rock-bottom, check the room first |
| **Kenya Comfort Hotel** | CBD (opposite Jeevanjee Gardens) | ~$18–45 depending on platform | Reliable, breakfast, close to the Arusha/Nairobi shuttle stop |

**Booking tips**
- Ask for a room **away from the street** (traffic noise in CBD is brutal from 5am).
- Confirm: hot water, mosquito net, working WiFi, and **a lockable store room or luggage room** — you'll be leaving stock while you shop.
- Ask whether they can receive/hold **parcels** for you (needed if a supplier delivers).
- Pay for the room in KES; keep the receipt.

## 5. Day 1 — Gikomba, start to finish

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| **05:00** | Wake. Light breakfast / mandazi + chai. Leave anything valuable in the hotel safe. Carry cash split in **3 pockets**, small denominations |
| **05:30** | Boda or Uber to **Gikomba** (KSh 200–400 from CBD). Arrive before the bales are opened |
| **06:00–06:30** | **The "camera" window.** When a new bale is opened, the first picks go to the first buyer — that's the best-quality batch and the priciest. Be present and ask who's opening what *(some traders reserve camera for regulars — befriend them, give your number)* |
| **06:30–10:00** | Main buying: ladies' tops, dresses, jeans, men's shirts, kids' wear. Walk several stalls before committing — quotes swing wildly for the same bale |
| **10:00–11:00** | Coffee/breakfast break; renegotiate the bales you walked away from |
| **11:00–13:00** | Second round: mix-and-match lots — prices drop when you buy 10–20 pcs per style; combine with **Kamukunji** for shoes |
| **13:00** | Lunch — nyama choma / chapati & beans nearby, KSh 300–600 |
| **14:00–16:30** | **Alpha Centre / Mombasa Road** if you're buying whole bales direct from importers, or head to **Eastleigh** to preview tomorrow's suppliers |
| **16:30–17:30** | **End-of-day "fagilia" trade** — leftovers dumped cheap (tops from ~KSh 15–25, dresses KSh 50–100 to bulk buyers). Best value of the day if you're flexible on styles |
| **17:30–18:30** | Get your stock out: Tuk-tuk/boda to the hotel store, **or straight to the bus parcel office** to ship (see §7). Don't leave stock in a market stall overnight |
| **19:30** | Dinner (KSh 400–800), repack/label everything, write supplier names + prices in a notebook, sleep early |

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| **06:00–09:00** | Back to Gikomba for anything you missed — or if you want **new** stock, go to **Eastleigh** instead (malls open ~8–9am) |
| **09:00–12:00** | Eastleigh wholesale circuit if doing new clothes: Al Arafat, Amal Plaza, Tansim, Olympic (shoes 1st floor wholesale), Prime, Pacific, New Mandara |

| **15:00–16:00** | Optional: Biashara Street (fabric) or a quick look at the CBD wholesale shops on Luthuli Ave / River Road |
| **16:00** | Head to JKIA (or the bus station). Allow **2.5h** for JKIA before an international flight in Nairobi traffic |
| **Evening** | Fly EBB, or night bus if going overland |

## 7. Getting your stock back to Kampala

**Cheapest reliable method:** bus-line parcel service (Easy Coach, Modern Coast, Kampala Coach and similar run daily on the Nairobi–Kampala route and carry parcels). Roughly KSh 500–1,500 per bale depending on size — **confirm and get a waybill/receipt**, and put your phone number on every package. Pack bales in the market's standard wrapping and mark them with your name + Kampala contact.

Alternatives: courier companies (door-to-door, pricier), or carrying it yourself as accompanied baggage (only sensible if it's small — see the customs note).

**Customs — read this before you commit big money:**
- Uganda's traveller allowance is **US$2,000 for personal-use goods** if you've been out of the country **more than 24 hours** (raised from $500 in July 2024). It **does not cover commercial goods or goods for resale** — and >50kg or obvious commercial quantity gets sent to the **cargo terminal** regardless of value.
- Formally, commercial imports are taxed: clothing attracts around **35% import duty + 18% VAT** and other EAC charges for non-EAC-origin goods (EAC-origin goods can qualify for preference with a certificate of origin — mitumba doesn't).
- The trade in **used clothing imports is politically sensitive** in Uganda/EAC and rules have shifted over the years. If you're importing commercial quantities, check the current position with **URA** (or use a licensed clearing agent) before you buy.
- Keep receipts for everything, declare honestly, and don't try to look like a personal traveller while carrying a commercial load.

| Hotel, 2 nights | $30–40 |
| Airport transfers | $15–25 |
| Local transport (2 days) | $6–12 |
| Meals (2 days) | $10–16 |
| Kenyan SIM + data | $2–5 |
| Freight of stock to Kampala | $5–15 per bale |
| **Total, excluding stock** | **≈ $265–420 by air · ≈ $105–170 by bus** |

**Trading budget:** a typical grade-1 bale runs **KSh 8,000–50,000** (premium up to 100,000+); basic grade-2 from ~KSh 3,000. Kids' bales ~KSh 6,000–17,000. Ladies' tops bales were around KSh 19,500 in late 2025 (up from ~12,000 in 2024), men's trouser bales ~KSh 25,000. **Half-bales** are common if you want to spread risk. Never blow your whole budget on day one.

*(Prices quoted in KES — check the current KSh/USD rate on the day; it has swung between roughly 129 and 160 in recent years.)*

## 9. Bargaining, safety & practical playbook

**Bargaining**
- The asking price is never the final price. Open at **one third** of the quote and work up. Buy in lots (10–20 pcs) to unlock wholesale rates.
- Reported on the ground: how you're **dressed** and the **language** you use affect the price — English gets a mark-up, Swahili/Sheng gets a fair price. Dress plainly, no jewellery, no flash.
- Inspect before paying. Ask whether a bale is **"tested/approved"**, ask to see the top layer and a random deeper layer, and check for mildew/damage. Get the seller's phone number and WhatsApp — repeat suppliers give you the camera pick.
- Don't buy from the first person who grabs you at the entrance; they're often brokers adding a margin.

**Safety**
- Gikomba is extremely crowded, has narrow lanes, and has suffered **major fire incidents** — keep escape routes in mind and don't leave stock stacked in the market.
- Watch for **pickpockets and phone snatchers**. Money split across pockets, no phone in hand while bargaining in the crowd.
- Use **Uber/Bolt or a known boda** after dark. Don't wander River Road at night.
- Ask at your hotel which gate/lane to enter the market from in the morning; the market has different reputations street by street.

**Health & documents**
- Carry your **passport or national ID** — Ugandan citizens enter Kenya without an eTA for up to 180 days (EAC exemption).
- Carry a valid **yellow fever vaccination card** — Kenya requires it for arrivals from Uganda, and you'll be asked on the way back too.
- Drink bottled water, eat cooked food, and carry ORS + basic meds. Nairobi is 1,700m up — the mornings are cold (12–15°C), so bring a light jacket.

**Carry:** passport/ID, yellow fever card, KES cash + a few USD for bureaus, phone (and a cheap spare if you can), power bank, a padlock for your bag, a small notebook for supplier details, and a printed/offline copy of this plan.

---

## 10. Do these three things before you travel

1. **Book the flight and hotel within 48 hours** — flight prices and the ~$18 rooms both move fast.
2. **Check the current Gikomba bale prices and URA rules** for whatever quantity you plan to import (see links below) — a 5-minute call to a Kenyan supplier or Ugandan clearing agent saves you real money.
3. **Line up 2–3 supplier contacts on WhatsApp before you land** (search "Gikomba bales" and "Eastleigh wholesale" on TikTok/Instagram — most traders list numbers). Arriving with contacts is what gets you the good bales at 6am.
