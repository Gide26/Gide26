## Abstract

This study assesses the impacts of Uganda's The New Vision newspaper across 40 years of publication (1986–2026) through four objectives: political-discourse influence, economic/industry contribution, social-development contribution, and digital-era sustainability. The design is a convergent mixed-methods case study: quantitative content analysis of 100 sampled editions stratified across four decades (1986–1995, 1996–2005, 2006–2015, 2016–2026), a survey of 40 readers, three key-informant interviews, and a platform-evolution audit. An explicit Theory of Change with falsification rules pre-specifies the causal chain. This proposal reports a real, verifiable archive dataset: 14 complete editions gathered from public sources (Wayback Machine snapshots, 17/01/2002–31/12/2019; live site 20/09/2026), yielding 132 coded items, each traceable to its archived URL. Preliminary results show politics/elections at 26% of items (28% even in the restricted Movement era), development-flagged content at 25% (varying 0–56% by edition type), stance toward state actors at 42% favourable / 36% neutral / 22% accountability-critical, women as confirmed sources/subjects in about 10% of items (half the industry baseline), and a directly observed 24-year digitalisation arc (e-mail bulletins and mass reader forums 2002–2005 → blogs/social 2007–2010 → apps/podcasts 2016 → paywall/multilingual 2026). The pilot establishes feasibility of the public-source protocol, shows print–online agenda divergence against prior monitoring, and identifies stratification corrections for the full study. Keywords: agenda-setting, framing, development media, political economy, digital transition, Uganda, New Vision, content analysis.
