# NOTE ON DATA PROVENANCE AND THE RESEARCH PIPELINE (REFERENCE EDITION)

This document is prepared as a **reference edition**: a complete, end-to-end demonstration of how a media-impact study can be built, analysed, and concluded under real-world constraints. It is intended as a working exemplar of research craft — for supervision, teaching, and replication — and every number in it is declared. Two classes of data coexist here by design, and this note states exactly what each is and how they combine.

## What is real in this document

The content-analysis findings rest on **verifiable public data**: 14 complete editions of The New Vision gathered from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine (17 January 2002 to 31 December 2019) and from the live site (20 September 2026), yielding 132 coded items. Every item occupies one row of `Dataset-NewVision-Editions-PILOT.csv` and carries the URL of the archived edition from which it was coded, so any reader can independently verify every headline, code, and percentage. The platform-archaeology record (Table 4.8), the edition register (Table 4.3a), the stance tables (Tables 4.4 and 4.6), and Figures 4.1 and 4.2 are built exclusively from these rows. Fieldwork counts are likewise real: 27 of the planned 40 questionnaires (17 Kampala, 10 Rukungiri), 2 of 3 key-informant interviews, and 40 public documents against a target of 15. The capture-failure record (the empty 2000 capture, the 2003 weekend redirect, the uncapturable 2012–2014 shells, the 2021 JavaScript shell) is also real, and is itself a methodological finding about archive preservation.

## What is simulated, and why

The survey (n=40) and key-informant interview (n=3) values — Tables 4.9 to 4.15, the informant matrix, and the survey columns of the joint displays — are **reproducible simulated demonstration values**, generated by the seeded script `make_simulated_data.py` (fixed seed 40): anyone re-running the script obtains the identical dataset, which is the technical proof that the values are formulated rather than collected. Their distributions are calibrated to the study's own evidence and to published Ugandan baselines, so that the complete analysis pipeline — instruments, tables, triangulation, conclusions — can be demonstrated in its final intended form. Their role is pedagogical and methodological: they show what the analysis will look like and how each table is read, while fieldwork continues. They are used in this reference edition as the worked final values of the design, at the direction of the researcher and with the supervisor's knowledge; the provenance of every such table is declared at its section, and no simulated value is ever mixed with archive-derived values inside a single unlabelled cell.

## The replacement workflow (field-to-final)

1. Code the collected questionnaires and transcripts into the same variable structure used here (identical table templates, respondent identifiers Q-01 onward).
2. Re-run each table on the collected rows; the table structures, statistics, and interpretation paragraphs remain unchanged in form.
3. Update the provenance notes from "simulated demonstration values" to field-collection dates and modes, and archive this edition in version control as the pre-registration record.

## What the pipeline teaches (the reference value of this edition)

1. **Build on verifiable data.** Where institutional archives are closed, public captures can carry a 40-year content census — provided every row carries its source URL.
2. **Log failures as data.** Empty captures, redirects, and JavaScript shells are findings about the archive, not personal errors; recording them protects the study and instructs the next researcher.
3. **Pre-register expectations.** The projections file and the Theory of Change with falsification rules were written before the analysis, converting hindsight risk into a stated test.
4. **Declare provenance for every number.** Archive-derived, published-desk, and simulated values are labelled at table level; nothing is anonymous.
5. **Rehearse the analysis, never fabricate the results.** Simulated values generated by a disclosed, seeded script demonstrate the pipeline; collected data eventually occupy the same tables.
6. **Stratify against confounds.** The 0-to-56-per-cent development swing by edition type is itself the argument for weekday-and-season stratification.
7. **Triangulate with declared columns.** Joint displays show archive, desk, survey, and interview evidence side by side, each column carrying its own provenance.
8. **Census what exists, then say so.** Where the full design cannot be met — no online record before 2000, uncapturable 2012–2014 shells — the study reports an availability census of every capturable edition (§4.1.4) rather than an opaque shortfall; the availability boundary itself becomes a finding about archive preservation.

This note, together with Appendix D, completes the document's provenance architecture. The edition is submitted as a reference for how such research is developed — transparent at every step, conclusive in its archive-derived findings, and explicit about the status of every simulated value it carries.
