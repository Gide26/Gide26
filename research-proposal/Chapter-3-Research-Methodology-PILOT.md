## 3.1 Introduction

This chapter describes a **pilot-scale mixed-methods assessment** of New Vision's 40-year impacts. A pilot is the honest and defensible choice here: with no guaranteed access to New Vision's archives or staff, this study demonstrates feasibility, tests all instruments, and produces **preliminary findings from public and secondary sources** plus small primary samples. Chapter Four therefore reports pilot results with explicit confidence limits, and Chapter Five includes a roadmap for scaling to the full study.

The design still answers the same four objectives (political, economic, social, digital impacts) and still traces every method to the Chapter Two theories and gaps (see Table 3.1), but each component is sized for **one researcher, 8–12 weeks, zero institutional access, minimal budget**.

## 3.2 Research Design

The study adopts a **convergent mixed-methods pilot case-study design** (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Yin, 2018): small quantitative components (100-edition content coding; n=40 survey) collected alongside qualitative depth (3 KIIs; open-ended survey items; document analysis), then triangulated. The logic is **retrospective longitudinal reconstruction**: four decade-strata (1986–1995; 1996–2005; 2006–2015; 2016–2026) sampled from whatever public holdings exist, with coverage rates transparently reported. Election windows (1996, 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016, 2021) are purposively included where holdings allow, supplemented by ACME's published monitoring datasets as secondary content evidence (ACME, 2016, 2021).

**Philosophy:** pragmatist — best-available-evidence answers from multiple paradigms, with claims calibrated to pilot sample sizes (descriptive, not inferential).

## 3.3 Study Area

- **Desk/content work:** Kampala-based public holdings — university libraries with New Vision bound volumes (researcher's own university library first; Makerere Africana and national/public libraries as backup), plus online sources (newvision.co.ug, Wayback Machine, AllAfrica).
- **Survey (n=40):** Kampala (n=25: e.g., campus + market/taxi-stage catchment) and **one up-country district** accessible to the researcher (n=15: trading centre + village). Exact sites to be named in the final thesis.
- **KIIs (3):** Kampala (in person or phone/Zoom).

## 3.4 Study Population

1. Publicly accessible New Vision content universe (library holdings + online + archived + legitimately republished).
2. Ugandan adults (18+) in the two survey sites who have ever regularly used any New Vision platform.
3. Publicly reachable expert informants (academics, retired journalists, media-NGO staff — **no current New Vision staff required**).
4. Public documents: USE/NVL disclosures, circulation audits, rate cards, ACME/UMWA/RSF/DataReportal/AMI.Barometer publications.

## 3.5 Sample Size

| Component | Pilot sample | Justification |
|---|---|---|
| Content analysis | **100 editions** (25 per decade: 5 years × 5 editions) | Largest feasible hand-coded sample for one researcher; adequate for descriptive decade comparisons with wide confidence intervals (reported as pilot estimates). |
| Audience survey | **n=40** (25 Kampala + 15 up-country) | Standard pilot-survey size; sufficient to test the instrument and report descriptive patterns. **No generalisation beyond the two sites.** |
| KIIs | **3** (1 journalism lecturer; 1 veteran/retired journalist; 1 media-NGO officer) | Maximum-variation mini-panel covering scholarship, practice, and watchdog perspectives. |
| Documents | Census of *accessible* public items (target: ≥15 substantive documents) | Availability sampling with a logged search trail. |
| Platform audit | 1-week snapshot of New Vision's public Facebook, X, YouTube (+ website) vs. 1 comparator | Demonstrates the audit protocol for the full study. |

## 3.6 Sampling Techniques and Procedures

### 3.6.1 Content sample (100 editions) — stratified purposive with public-source protocol
1. **Stratify by decade** (25 each). Within each decade, select **5 sample years** spread across the decade **plus** any election year falling in it (e.g., Decade 2: 1996, 1998, 2000, 2002, 2005).
2. Within each sample year, select **5 editions** on fixed constructed days (e.g., 2nd Monday of Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct + 1 election-month front page where applicable). If the exact date is unavailable, take the nearest available edition and **log the substitution**.
3. **Source ladder (use in this order; log which rung each edition came from):**
   - **Rung 1 — Library bound volumes** (primary for 1986–2005): researcher's university library → Makerere Africana → public/national libraries. Photograph/scan front page + editorials + sampled inside pages (permission permitting; for fair-dealing research use).
   - **Rung 2 — Wayback Machine** (`web.archive.org`, domain `newvision.co.ug`) for 2000s–2020s: pick archived snapshots nearest the target dates; capture homepage + top 10 stories.
   - **Rung 3 — Live website archive/search** (`newvision.co.ug`) for recent years: sample via site search/date filters.
   - **Rung 4 — AllAfrica.com and Google-indexed reprints** of New Vision stories to fill thematic gaps (code as *reprint*, not edition).
   - **Rung 5 — Secondary content datasets** (ACME 2016/2021 monitoring tables; UMWA 2016 gender tables): analyse as published data, clearly labelled secondary.
4. **Within-edition coding:** front page (all stories) + editorials/op-eds + health/education/development items anywhere in the edition (thematic flag). Inside-page random sampling is dropped at pilot scale — code what's accessible and report the denominator honestly.
5. **Coverage log:** a master table (date sought → date obtained → rung → condition) becomes Appendix Table 1 and the basis of the §4.1 coverage report. **Missing early editions are expected; the missingness pattern is itself reported.**

### 3.6.2 Survey (n=40) — purposive quota + convenience
- Quotas: gender 20/20; age 18–30 (14), 31–50 (14), 51+ (12); site 25/15. Recruit at public catchment points (campus, market, stage, trading centre) with a screening question ("Have you ever regularly read/watched/listened to/followed any New Vision paper, station, or page?"). One respondent per household. Contribution: transport refund only (no inducement).

### 3.6.3 KIIs (3) — purposive
- KII-1: journalism/media lecturer (theory + history perspective).
- KII-2: veteran or retired journalist with pre-2000s memory (practice perspective; former New Vision staff ideal **only if reachable publicly** — no institutional approach).
- KII-3: media-NGO programme officer (e.g., ACME/UMWA/HRNJ-U alumni networks — watchdog perspective).
- 30–45 minutes, semi-structured, audio-recorded with consent, in person or remote.

## 3.7 Data Collection Methods

1. **Structured content analysis** (quantitative-dominant): issue salience, actors, sources, stance, development-news flag, Entman-lite frame codes (problem/cause/moral/solution present Y/N + dominant generic frame) — one coding sheet per edition (see Pilot Instruments).
2. **Face-to-face survey** (15–20 min): repertoire, gratifications (10-item short battery), trust, agenda ("most important problem"), health/education recall, digital module, memorable-impact open end.
3. **Semi-structured KIIs** (3): decade-anchored recall + political-economy probes + digital verdict + "what must the full study not miss."
4. **Document review + mini platform audit:** extraction checklist per document; one-week public metrics snapshot (followers, post frequency, engagement on top posts, Luganda-vs-English mix) for New Vision vs. one comparator (e.g., Daily Monitor).

## 3.8 Research Instruments

Four tested instruments (see `Pilot-Instruments-Print-Ready.md`): (1) one-page **Edition Coding Sheet** + codebook; (2) **Survey Questionnaire** (4 sections, Luganda + English); (3) **KII Guide** (10 core questions + role probes); (4) **Document Extraction + Platform Audit Sheet**. All instruments will be **pretested** (5 editions double-coded; 5 pilot questionnaires off-sample; 1 mock interview) and revised before main pilot fieldwork.

## 3.9 Validity, Reliability and Quality Control

- **Content coding:** researcher + 1 trained assistant independently double-code **20 editions (20%)**; target Cohen's κ ≥ 0.70 (≥ 0.60 minimum on frame variables); adjudicate and report κ per variable in §4.1.
- **Survey:** 10-item gratification battery adapted from Katz et al. (1973) and Kalyango & Onyechi (2013); pretest n=5 with cognitive debriefing; back-translation check for Luganda; supervisor back-checks 10% by phone.
- **Qualitative trustworthiness** (Lincoln & Guba, 1985): triangulation across the four methods; member-checking (KII summaries returned to all 3 informants); reflexivity journal; audit trail of sampling substitutions.
- **Secondary-data discipline:** every desk statistic in Chapter Four carries (a) source, (b) year, (c) original methodology note, and (d) comparability warning where methods changed (e.g., DataReportal's 2023→2025 social-media methodology shift — see §4.6).

## 3.10 Data Analysis

- **Quantitative (Excel/SPSS):** frequencies, percentages, and clustered bar charts by decade/title; issue-share and actor-share tables; survey descriptives with site/age/gender splits. **No inferential statistics beyond the pilot's power** — differences are reported as *observed pilot patterns* with raw counts always shown beside percentages (so examiners see the small-n basis).
- **Qualitative (manual thematic tables; NVivo optional):** deductive skeleton from Chapter Two (development roles, agenda cues, ownership pressures, Entman functions, gratifications) + inductive codes; one joint display per objective pairing the strongest count with the strongest quote/document extract.
- **Integration rule:** no finding is stated on a single source type alone where triangulation was possible; convergences and divergences are explicitly noted.

## 3.11 Ethical Considerations

1. **Clearances:** university faculty/REC approval per UCU/KIU student-research requirements (confirm whether UNCST clearance is required for a student pilot — many Ugandan universities exempt small student pilots; obtain the exemption letter if so). No New Vision access sought, so no corporate permission required; current New Vision staff will **not** be approached.
2. **Consent:** voluntary written/oral consent in the respondent's language; separate recording consent; right to skip/withdraw.
3. **Confidentiality:** anonymous questionnaires (no names; phones stored separately for FGD follow-up only); KIIs default to role-only attribution with graded options (anonymous/role/named); encrypted storage; anonymised quotes.
4. **Sensitivity:** political questions framed historically/comparatively; private interview settings; no identifiable attribution of critical claims; findings worded to avoid exposing individuals.
5. **Archival/copyright:** library rules observed; scans for fair-dealing research use only; no republication of full editions.
6. **Reciprocity:** one-page Luganda/English summary shared with survey sites on request; findings brief offered to the 3 informants.

## 3.12 Limitations of the Pilot (and Full-Study Roadmap)

1. **Small samples (100/40/3):** findings are *preliminary and site-bound*; the thesis states this on every findings table ("pilot estimates — do not generalise").
2. **No archive/staff access:** pre-2000 coverage will be thin; political-economy claims rest on public documents + 3 external informants, not newsroom insiders.
3. **Convenience survey:** Kampala + 1 district, non-random — patterns are illustrative, and the validated questionnaire is itself a pilot output for the full study.
4. **Recall bias:** mitigated by content-first triangulation.
5. **Causality:** the study establishes *plausible contribution*, not causation ("consistent with," "suggests").
6. **Roadmap:** §5.5 converts every limitation into a full-study specification (archival census, n=768 survey, 35–45 KIIs, newsroom access) — the pilot's strategic function.
