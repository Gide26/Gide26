## RE: SUBMISSION OF RESEARCH PROPOSAL AND REQUEST FOR APPROVAL TO PROCEED TO FIELDWORK — "ASSESSING THE IMPACTS OF THE NEW VISION NEWSPAPER OVER THE PAST 40 YEARS OF ITS EXISTENCE (1986–2026): A MIXED-METHODS PILOT STUDY"

I write to submit the above-titled research proposal, prepared under your guidance, for your review and approval to proceed to the fieldwork phase.

**The study.** The proposal assesses the 40-year impacts of Uganda's longest continuously publishing national newspaper through four objectives: (1) influence on political discourse; (2) economic and industry contribution; (3) social-development contribution; and (4) digital-era sustainability. The design is a convergent mixed-methods case study: quantitative content analysis of 100 editions stratified across four decades (25 per decade, 1986–2026), a survey of 40 readers, three key-informant interviews, and a platform-evolution audit — all disciplined by an explicit Theory of Change with pre-specified assumptions and falsification rules. Referencing is in APA 7th edition.

**Work completed to date.** Beyond the proposal text (Chapters One to Five complete, with print-ready instruments), I have built a real, verifiable pilot dataset: **14 complete editions coded (n=132 items, 2002–2026)**, gathered from public archives (Wayback Machine snapshots and the live site), with every coded item traceable to its archived URL. Preliminary findings already anchor Chapter Four: politics/elections at 26% of items, development-flagged content at 25%, stance toward state actors at 42% favourable / 36% neutral / 22% accountability-critical, women as sources/subjects in about 10% of items, and a directly observed 24-year digitalisation arc (reader forums from 2003 to a paywall in 2026).

**One practical constraint discovered during the pilot.** No online record of the newspaper exists before 8 March 2000 (the Internet Archive's earliest capture), and the 2012–2014 website years are technically uncapturable. The first decades therefore require access to the **library's bound volumes and the print archive**, which shapes the fieldwork plan below.

**Requests.** Kindly: (i) approve the proposal for progression; (ii) advise on the Research Ethics Committee application, which I will file before any human-subject data collection; (iii) provide an endorsement letter for archive access (New Vision library/bound volumes, 1986–1999 and 2012–2014); and (iv) permit commencement of the content-coding phase, followed by the survey (n=40) and key-informant interviews (3) once ethical clearance is granted.
