## 5.3 Conclusions
Organised by (a) empirical conclusions, (b) theoretical conclusions, (c) methodological reflections:

- **5.3.1 Empirical conclusions:** the overall 40-year verdict — e.g., indispensable development communicator with constrained democratic contribution? Multimedia success facing sustainability cliff? (To be written from evidence; avoid pre-judging in proposal.)
- **5.3.2 Theoretical conclusions:** what does New Vision's 40 years teach us about each Chapter Two theory?
  - Development Media Theory: vindicated, qualified, or refuted in the Ugandan case? (cf. Servaes, 1999; Wasserman, 2011 participatory reformulations.)
  - Agenda-Setting: does a state-majority daily still set the agenda in a social-media age? (cf. Kalyango & Onyechi, 2013; Banda, 2009 limits.)
  - Political Economy: how did ownership/advertising mechanisms operate in practice? (cf. Herman & Chomsky, 1988; Mwesige, 2004; Nassanga, 2008.)
  - Framing: which frames endured 40 years and which died? (cf. Entman, 1993.)
  - Uses & Gratifications: how did audience agency reshape intended impacts? (cf. Katz et al., 1973.)
- **5.3.3 Contribution to knowledge:** state plainly what is now known that was not known before (the 5 gaps closed).
