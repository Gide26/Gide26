## Appendix B: Key Informant Interview Guides

**Core module (all informants, ~30–45 min):**
1. Timeline anchor: When did you first encounter/work with New Vision? Walk me through the decades you know best.
2. Political role: How would you describe New Vision's political role in [decade they know]? Give one example of strong journalism and one of weak/compromised coverage.
3. Elections: How was election coverage decided in your time? Who was in the room? (Probe gently: ownership signals, advertiser pressure, self-censorship.)
4. Development role: What was New Vision's best development contribution (health/education/unity)? Evidence?
5. Economics: How did money shape journalism — advertising, government supplements, expos, printing contracts?
6. Language/regions: Who was included/excluded by language and distribution choices?
7. Digital: When did digital "hit" New Vision? What worked, what failed, why?
8. Comparison: Compared to Monitor/UBC/regional state media, where does New Vision stand?
9. Future: What must change for New Vision to thrive the NEXT 10 years?
10. Records: What documents/archives should this study not miss? Who else must I interview?

**Role modules (add 4–6 questions each):**
- *Editors/reporters (current/former, decade-stratified):* newsroom routines, story-kill examples, source-protection, gender in newsroom, training.
- *Managers/commercial:* business model history, USE listing effects, circulation/revenue trends, rate-setting, expo economics.
- *Government/regulators:* mandate expectations, subsidy vs. independence views, regulation history.
- *Opposition/civil society:* bias documentation, access-to-coverage experiences, reform demands.
- *Advertisers/media buyers:* spend decisions, boycotts or threats, rate vs. influence perceptions.
- *Educators (Makerere/UCU/KIU):* graduate quality, curriculum use of New Vision, research gaps.
- *Digital/tech staff:* metrics access, CMS/social workflows, verification vs. speed, monetisation attempts.
- *Archivists/librarians:* holdings completeness, preservation state, access rules.

**Closing:** graded-anonymity choice ([ ] anonymous [ ] role-only [ ] named), member-check consent, document leads, thanks.

- Stance toward government (political stories): Favourable / Neutral-balanced / Unfavourable / N-A
- Coder ID | Difficult-call note

**Sheet B — Frames (political/election stories):**
- Entman functions (open-code short phrases): Problem definition | Causal interpretation | Moral evaluation | Treatment recommendation
- Generic frames (0=absent 1=present 2=dominant): Conflict / Responsibility / Human-interest / Economic-consequences / Development-progress / National-unity / Liberation-legacy / Sectarianism-threat
- Opposition frame: Legitimate-contender / Troublemaker / Puppet-foreign / Victim / Invisible-token
- Government frame: Developer-deliverer / Unifier / Security-guarantor / Accountable / Criticised-failing
- Actor–issue pairs (for L3 network coding): list up to 3 pairs per story

**Sheet C — Development themes (health/education/unity items):**
- Campaign linkage (HIV/AIDS phases, UPE, malaria, immunisation, maternal, COVID, other)
- Tone: Fear-alarm / Empowerment-actionable / Informational-neutral / Moralising / Stigmatising (flag)
- Actionability: specific behaviour/service info present? (Y/N) | Sources: health officials / medics / patients / NGOs / citizens
- Unity items: ethnic/religious framing (bridging / neutral / divisive-flag) | Language of publication

**Reliability:** 10% overlap double-coding; Krippendorff's α targets in §3.9.1; adjudication log.

## Appendix D: FGD Guide (8 groups, 90 min, preferred language)

1. Opening + timeline exercise: "Draw or narrate New Vision's history as YOU remember it — when did it matter most to your community?" (20 min participatory)
2. Uses: "What do you personally use New Vision for? What does it do that nothing else does?" (gratifications probe)
3. Politics: "When elections come, do you trust what New Vision says? Why/why not?" (generational probe: do elders and youth see it differently?)
4. Development: "Has New Vision ever helped your family with health, farming, school, or business? Tell the story."
5. Language: "Does New Vision speak YOUR language — literally and culturally? Who is left out?"
6. Money: "New Vision is government-owned but sells adverts — does that affect what it says? Examples?"
7. Digital: "Print, radio, TV, phone — where is New Vision in your life now vs. 10 years ago? What would make you pay for it online?"
8. Closing: "New Vision at 50 — what should it look like?" + thanks + transport refund + summary-sharing consent.

*Composition (8–10 each): (1) urban youth 18–30; (2) rural youth; (3) women mixed-age; (4) elders 50+; (5) Luganda readers; (6) regional-language readers; (7) digital-only users; (8) vendors/distributors.*

**E2. Platform audit (quarterly × 4 for 12-month series + competitor snapshot):**
