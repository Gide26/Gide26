## 4.3 Findings on Objective 1: Political Impacts (Democratic Governance, Elections, Public Discourse)
*Theories: Agenda-Setting (3 levels), Framing (Entman), Political Economy. Methods: content analysis + frame analysis + survey agenda items + political KIIs/FGDs.*

- 4.3.1 First-level agenda-setting: issue-salience trends across 4 decades (stacked time-series: politics vs. development vs. social vs. sport/entertainment). Election-period spikes. *Bukedde* vs. *New Vision* agenda comparison.
- 4.3.2 Second-level agenda-setting / framing: dominant frames per election (1996–2021) mapped to Entman's 4 functions; stance distributions (pro-government/neutral/pro-opposition) with decade trends; emergent Uganda-specific frames.
- 4.3.3 Third-level agenda-setting: actor–issue co-occurrence networks (e.g., Opposition↔conflict vs. Government↔development) for anniversary subsamples.
- 4.3.4 Voice and source diversity: government vs. opposition vs. civil-society vs. citizen sourcing over time; women as sources; language/title differences.
- 4.3.5 Public-agenda correlation: survey "most important problem" vs. coded New Vision agenda (Spearman ρ) — direct agenda-setting test.
- 4.3.6 Political-economy mechanisms: KII evidence on ownership pressure, editorial autonomy episodes, advertiser influence, self-censorship; critical incidents (to be anonymised).
- 4.3.7 Audience verdict: survey trust/credibility + FGD narratives on bias vs. balance; generational differences.
- 4.3.8 Interpretation: What do 4.3.1–4.3.7 jointly imply about New Vision's political impacts? Convergence/divergence joint display.

## 4.4 Findings on Objective 2: Economic Impacts (Industry, Advertising, Employment, Knowledge Economy)
*Theory: media economics (Picard; Doyle) + Political Economy. Methods: document/financial review + economic KIIs + survey employment/advertising items.*

- 4.4.1 Corporate growth: revenue/masthead timeline (indexed series), USE-listing effects (2004), diversification (print→broadcast→digital→expos→printing).
- 4.4.2 Employment: headcount time-series (direct), wage-bill where available, job categories, gender patterns; indirect employment (vendors, distributors, agents, stringers).
- 4.4.3 Advertising-market development: rate-card evolution, advertiser counts, market-share estimates, role in professionalising media-buying; triangulated buyer testimony.
- 4.4.4 Printing and value chain: commercial-print revenues, equipment/technology spillovers, paper/distribution linkages.
- 4.4.5 Talent pipeline ("school of journalism"): alumni tracing — New Vision-trained journalists now leading other outlets; training investments.
- 4.4.6 Sustainability stress: print-circulation/revenue decline indicators, cost pressures, digital-revenue share; scenario note.
- 4.4.7 Interpretation: net economic assessment — contributions vs. vulnerabilities.

## 4.5 Findings on Objective 3: Social and Development Impacts (Health, Education, National Unity)
*Theory: Development Media Theory criteria + health/education literature (§2.8). Methods: thematic content analysis + audience survey Sec. D + health/teacher KIIs + campaign documents.*

- 4.5.1 Health communication: HIV/AIDS coverage intensity 1987–2005 (article counts, front-page share, tone: fear vs. empowerment vs. informational); malaria/immunisation/maternal-health series; COVID-19 coverage audit (2020–2022). Health-worker KII testimony on usefulness. Audience recall of health behaviours linked to New Vision.
- 4.5.2 Education and literacy: education-supplement history, school-challenge/expo programmes, literacy-accessibility (language levels, multilingual reach); teacher/learner testimony.
- 4.5.3 National unity and language inclusion: multilingual-title history and reach (Bukedde, Rupiny, Etop, Orumuri); framing of ethnicity/religion/conflict (unity vs. sensationalism coding); Northern-Uganda FGD perspectives.
- 4.5.4 Development-news share: proportion of development-oriented content by decade — direct Development Media Theory test (cf. §2.2.1 expectations).
- 4.5.5 Interpretation: where did New Vision meet, exceed, or betray development-media expectations? (Link to Servaes/Wasserman participatory critique, §2.2.1.)

## 4.6 Findings on Objective 4: Digital Transformation and Future Sustainability
*Theory: digital transformation (§2.7) + Uses & Gratifications (platform migration). Methods: digital analytics + platform audit + survey Sec. E + digital-staff KIIs + FGD digital narratives.*

- 4.6.1 Digital footprint history: website → e-paper → apps → social accounts timeline with follower/user growth curves.
- 4.6.2 Current performance: 12-month web/social metrics dashboard; engagement rates; video vs. text; Luganda vs. English digital performance.
- 4.6.3 Audience migration: survey platform repertoires by generation; digital-only vs. print-loyal segments; willingness-to-pay results; FGD migration narratives ("why I left print" / "why I stay").
- 4.6.4 Newsroom adaptation: digital-workflow evidence (convergence, verification practices, social-listening); Beckett's "networked journalism" test — does New Vision practice it?
- 4.6.5 Business-model audit: digital-revenue streams, e-paper/paywall performance, social-monetisation limits; competitor benchmarking (Monitor, UBC, pure-digital players); AMI (2016) "no sustainable model" test.
- 4.6.6 Digital divide limits: Wasserman (2011) inequality check — who is excluded from New Vision digital (rural, women, low-literacy)?
- 4.6.7 Interpretation: transformed, transforming, or stuck? Sustainability verdict with scenarios.
