## 3. Negotiate — this is what decides your profit

Their first price is a **retail** price. You're buying 30+ pieces. Push:

| Item | They'll ask | **Your target (30+ pcs)** |
|---|---|---|
| Textured / fashion polo | KSh 1,200 | **KSh 750–900** |
| Cuban / Chinese collar shirt | KSh 1,200–1,500 | **KSh 800–1,000** |
| Cargo pants | KSh 900–1,600 | **KSh 650–800** |
| Jeans (new) | KSh 1,400–1,500 | **KSh 1,000–1,200** |
| Plain tee | KSh 350 | **KSh 250** |

**Say:** *"Nataka bei ya wholesale — nataka pieces thelathini. Bei gani?"*
(Wholesale price — I want thirty pieces. What price?)

**Then walk.** In Eastleigh, starting to leave is the single most effective negotiating move. There are 20 shops selling the same stock.

**This is the truth you need before you spend:** on the fashion route you roughly **break even**, because you're buying at close to the same prices the Kampala shops buy at — and then paying 59.3% duty on top.

**On godown basics you'd clear ~UGX 1,143,000.** The fashion items look right but pay far less.

### Why — and how to fix it

The Kampala accounts you screenshotted sell those shirts at UGX 45,000. **That IS roughly their cost.** There is no fat in that line — unless you buy at genuine wholesale volume (MOQ 100+) or go direct to an importer.

Two fixes available to you:
1. **Negotiate to the target prices above** — every KSh 100 off adds UGX 3,020 of profit per piece.
2. **Buy fewer, better pieces and sell at a higher price.** A good textured polo can go at UGX 55,000–60,000 if it's genuinely nice, not UGX 45,000.

## 7. Straight talk

You can absolutely buy the items in your pictures — but be clear-eyed: **they're a break-even line on this trip**, not a profit line. The reason is that Nairobi retail prices and Kampala retail prices are nearly the same for branded-look fashion.

If your goal is **money**, buy tees and basics.
If your goal is **stock that looks like your pictures**, buy the fashion items — but buy fewer, negotiate ruthlessly, and keep the tees alongside them.

**Don't spend KSh 27,000 on fashion items and arrive in Kampala with a suitcase of near-cost goods and no duty money.**
