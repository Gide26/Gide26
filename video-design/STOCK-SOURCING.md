# Stock Sourcing Guide
### *Umuriro w'Urwanda* — how to actually source all 63 shots from licensed libraries

Companion to `assets/stock-shot-list.csv`, which carries per-shot keywords, suggested
libraries, frame-rate notes, licence cautions and a fallback for every shot.

## 2. The problem at the centre of this film

Real Intore and Umushagiriro performance footage in stock libraries is overwhelmingly:

- **Editorial Use Only** — recorded at public events without model releases
- **Not model released** — you cannot use identifiable faces in advertising
- **Low frame rate** — 25 or 29.97 fps, which kills the slow-motion design
- **Low resolution** — 1080p, not 4K
- **Poorly keyed** — tagged "tribal dance Africa" rather than "Intore Rwanda"

A representative real example: a 22-second Intore spear-dance clip on Pond5, shot in Musanze,
is **1920×1080, 29.97 fps, marked `Usage: Editorial`, `Model Released: false`**. That single
metadata line disqualifies it from any commercial, brand or tourism-board delivery, and its
frame rate disqualifies it from the 0.35× slow-motion the sequence is designed around.

### What "Editorial Use Only" means for you

| Delivery | Editorial clips usable? |
|---|---|
| Personal portfolio / showreel, non-monetised | Usually yes — check the specific licence |
| Documentary, news, educational, non-profit cultural | Usually yes |
| **Tourism-board campaign, brand ad, sponsored content** | **No** |
| **Anything monetised on YouTube/TikTok with brand partnership** | **No** |
| Film festival entry (non-commercial) | Usually yes |

> **Action:** in every library, **filter by licence before you filter by relevance.**
> Falling in love with an editorial clip and then discovering it in minute 40 of the search
> is the single biggest time-waste in stock-sourced editing.

### The three ways out — pick one now, before you search

**Option 1 — License direct from Rwandan sources (best result, moderate effort).**
This is the option I recommend for this film. The people who own the best footage are the
troupes and institutions themselves, and they are frequently open to licensing:

- **Rwanda Cultural Heritage Academy** — the authority on Intore costume and choreography
- **Urukerereza** (the Rwandan National Ballet) — professional performance archive
- **Inganzo Ngari** (Kigali, founded 2006) — large troupe, mixed-gender, high production value
- **Ingoma Nshya** (founded 2002) — the women's drumming ensemble; ideal for 4.11 and 4.12
- **Rwanda Development Board (RDB)** — holds and commissions tourism footage; may license or
  supply for a project that promotes Rwanda
- Individual Rwandan videographers on Pond5, Artlist, Vimeo Stock and Instagram — often
  happy to license out-of-catalogue material directly, and they have the 4K/60 fps material
  the libraries do not

Approach: describe the project, the deliverable, the territory and the term, and ask for a
**written licence with model releases confirmed**. Offer credit. Budget honestly — a direct
licence from a professional troupe for a 60-second commercial film is a real fee, but it is
usually comparable to buying 8–12 premium per-clip licences, and the footage is better.

**Option 2 — Substitute gracefully (fastest, no rights risk).**
Take the substitutions in `assets/stock-shot-list.csv` column `fallback_if_unavailable`.
You lose cultural specificity in roughly 12 shots; you keep the film's rhythm, beauty and
emotional arc intact. Most viewers will not notice that the flowing fabric in 4.03 is not an
*umushanana*. **Rwandan viewers will notice**, and so will a Rwandan client — which is why
this option is only safe for a non-Rwandan-audience, non-Rwandan-client delivery.

**Option 3 — Hybrid (what I would actually do).**
License the dance directly (Option 1) for Acts 4A and 4B only — about 19 shots — and buy
everything else from libraries (Option 2). The dance is the film's subject; the landscape and
the recreation are its setting, and setting footage is commodity.

## 3. Library strategy

### Which library for which act

| Library | Model | Best for here | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Artgrid / Artlist Max** | Subscription, unlimited downloads, royalty-free, commercial use covered | **Acts 1, 3, 5, 6** — landscape, recreation, portraits, fire, water | The best cinematic quality per dollar. Shot in story sets by the same DPs, so clips from one collection match each other — critical for the match-cut spine. 4K/8K, and RAW/LOG on higher tiers for grading. Verify current pricing on their site. |
| **Filmsupply** | Per-clip, premium | The five hero shots: 3.07, 4.05, 5.01, 6.04, 6.07 | Most expensive, most beautiful. Native slow motion. Read the licence carefully — it is more restrictive than Artgrid's. |
| **Pond5** | Per-clip marketplace, up to ~$1M indemnification on premium licences | **Act 4 dance**, imigongo, inyambo cattle, anything Rwanda-specific | Largest marketplace for specific, rare, regional content, including Rwandan contributors. Also the most likely place to find Intore at all — and the most likely place for it to be editorial-only. |
| **Getty / iStock** | Per-clip and packs | Act 4 dance, portraits with clean model releases | Deepest editorial archive and the strongest release discipline. Pricey. |
| **Shutterstock** | Subscription + per-clip | Volume fill: crowds, hands, clapping, generic recreation | Enormous, uneven quality. Good for Tier-A shots at low cost. |
| **Storyblocks / Envato Elements** | Cheap subscription, unlimited | Fill and outtakes | Fine for B-roll you'll only see for 0.5 s. Weak on anything Rwanda-specific. |
| **raw.film / FilmPac / Dissolve** | Subscription or per-clip | Gradeable LOG landscape for Acts 1, 5, 6 | If you want real colour control rather than baked-in looks. |
| **Pexels / Pixabay / Mixkit / Videvo** | Free, commercial use permitted | Zero-budget version | Rwanda coverage is thin. Usable for landscape and recreation; you will not find Intore. |
| **UNESCO archive** | Rights held by UNESCO; request licensing | Reference, and possibly the 2024 Intore inscription B-roll | UNESCO published an Intore B-roll reel alongside the 2024 intangible-heritage inscription. Contact them about licensing — it is authoritative material. |

### Search technique that actually works

1. **Search in three registers per shot.** English generic ("African warrior dance leap"),
   English specific ("Intore dance Rwanda"), and **Kinyarwanda** ("Intore", "Umushagiriro",
   "umuhamirizo", "ingoma", "inyambo", "imigongo", "agaseke", "umushanana", "amayugi").
   Kinyarwanda keywords are the single highest-yield trick — Rwandan contributors tag in
   their own language and Western searchers never find those clips.
2. **Search by contributor, not just by keyword.** Once you find one good Rwandan clip, open
   that contributor's portfolio. One DP who shot an Intore performance probably shot 40 usable
   angles of it — and those 40 angles are what makes the match-cut spine possible, because
   they share a lens, a colour temperature and a light.
3. **Buy collections, not clips.** For Acts 1, 5 and 6, get everything from one Artgrid
   collection so the grain, contrast and skin tones match before you grade.
4. **Always download the highest available resolution**, even if you're delivering 1080p —
   you need the pixels for the crop-in builds (2.06, 4.02, 4.09, 4.10).
5. **Preview at 100%, not fit-to-screen.** Stock is full of soft focus and compression
   artefacts you cannot see in a thumbnail grid.
6. **Save shortlists with the shot ID in the note field** — `4.04-candidate-3` — so the
   search and the assembly never get disconnected.

## 5. Frame rate — the trap nobody warns you about

The design sequence was written for footage you shoot at 120–240 fps. **Stock is overwhelmingly
24, 25 or 30 fps.** This is the biggest practical problem in a stock-sourced version, bigger
than the licensing.

What happens if you ignore it: CapCut's `Smooth slow-mo → Optical Flow` synthesises
in-between frames. From 30 fps pushed to 0.35× it is inventing **roughly 80% of the frames**,
and it fails predictably on exactly the material this film is made of — flying sisal fibres,
splashing water, patterned beadwork, fast limbs. You get smearing, ghosting and warped hands.

### The rules

| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| Shot needs to go below 0.5× | **Buy native slow motion** (clip labelled 100/120/240 fps or "slow motion"). Applies to 3.07, 4.05, 5.01, 4.04, 4.17. |
| Native slow motion unavailable | **Don't go below 0.5×.** Soften the ramp. A 0.5× ramp from 30 fps with Optical Flow is usually acceptable; 0.35× is not. |
| Optical Flow smears on fibres or water | Switch `Smooth slow-mo` to **`Frame blending`**. Frame blending gives a natural motion-blur ghost instead of a warp. On flying sisal it often looks *better* than reality. |
| Clip is 25 fps, project is 60 fps | CapCut duplicates frames. It is fine for 0.5 s cuts; it is visible on anything held. Prefer 30 fps sources for held shots. |
| You must hold a slow shot and only have 30 fps | Use a **freeze frame** instead of slow motion. `Freeze` for 8–12 frames on the peak of the action, then release. It reads as a deliberate stylistic choice rather than a technical failure — and it is exactly the freeze-and-release move already used on the drops. |

### Shots to re-spec if you cannot get native slow motion

| Shot | Original | Re-spec |
|---|---|---|
| 4.01 Umushagiriro | 0.4× Optical Flow | 0.6× Optical Flow, extend the clip to 1.6 s so the act still totals 3.5 s |
| 4.02 Hands | 0.4× | **Cut it.** Replace with a crop-in on 4.01, at 1.0×, keyframed Scale 100→190 |
| 4.03 Fabric | 0.3× | 0.5× Frame blending |
| 4.05 Landing | 0.2× for 5 frames | 4-frame **freeze** on the impact + Shake 12 + the real stomp audio. Arguably harder-hitting than the ramp. |
| 4.17 Mane | 0.3× | 0.5× Frame blending, Glow 20 |
| 5.01 Exhale | 0.35× over 3.0 s | 0.6× over 2.0 s + a 1.0 s **freeze** on the smile. Protects the moment without inventing 80% of the frames. |

## 6. Match cuts from stock — why they break, and how to fix them

The match-cut spine assumes you controlled both sides of every cut. You didn't. Two clips
from two contributors will not share a shape, a screen size, a lens or a colour temperature.

**Buy for shape, not for content.** For each match, what must align is:

| Match | What must actually match |
|---|---|
| bells → bike wheel | A **circular object centred in frame**, similar screen size, similar rotation direction |
| shield → paddle splash | A **circle**, and a similar distance from the lens |
| spiral → turning wrist | A **curved motion path** across frame |
| gorilla's eye → dancer's face | **Direct eye contact with the lens**, similar focal length, similar face size in frame |
| mane → tea terraces | **Fine texture rippling horizontally**, similar scale, similar backlight |
| dusk valley → dawn valley | **Identical composition** — ideally the same clip, regraded |

**Fixes when they don't match:**

1. **Reframe by cropping.** Keyframe `Scale` and `Position` so both shots present the shared
   shape at the same size and position. Only works if you bought 4K for a 1080p delivery.
2. **Bridge with a wipe.** `Mask → Circle` on the incoming clip, keyframed open over 12 frames,
   hides almost any mismatch. This is Method A in the playbook, and it becomes your most-used tool.
3. **Insert a third shot.** A 4-frame abstract (dust, light leak, blurred motion) between two
   mismatched shots makes the eye accept the pairing.
4. **Drop the match.** Cut on the beat instead. The spine is a bonus, not a contract — a
   well-timed hard cut is never worse than a forced match cut.

## 7. Colour matching mismatched stock

Sixty-three clips from eight libraries will not look like one film until you force them to.

1. Grade with **adjustment clips on V5, one per act** (see the playbook §6). This is
   non-negotiable with stock — it is what turns 63 clips into one film.
2. Use desktop `Adjust → Colour Match` with a reference clip per act. Pick your best-looking
   clip as the reference and match the others to it.
3. **Neutralise before you stylise.** Many clips arrive with a baked-in teal-and-orange look.
   Pull `Saturation` down to −10 and `Temp` toward 0 first, then apply the act grade on top.
4. **Add grain globally.** `Effects → Retro → Film Grain` at 12–18 on V2 across the entire
   film. This is the single most effective trick for unifying clips shot on different cameras
   at different bitrates — it gives them one shared texture.
5. **Upscale the weak clips.** CapCut Pro's video enhancement/upscaling helps 1080p stock sit
   beside 4K. Apply it to individual clips, not the whole timeline, and compare before/after —
   it can oversharpen faces.
6. Watch for **flicker** in auto-exposed stock (drone and handheld clips especially). Fix with
   a `Keyframe` on `Exposure` that counter-drifts, or replace the clip.

## 8. Licensing checklist — before you publish

**Per clip**
- [ ] Licence type recorded: Royalty-Free / Extended / Rights-Managed / Editorial-only
- [ ] Commercial use permitted for **this** delivery (brand, tourism board, monetised channel)
- [ ] Model release confirmed if any face is identifiable
- [ ] Property release confirmed if any recognisable building, artwork or logo appears
- [ ] Territory and term cover the intended distribution
- [ ] Resolution and frame rate are what you thought you bought
- [ ] Licence certificate / invoice PDF saved in `00_LICENSES/` beside the project

**Per platform**
- [ ] Music licence is separate from the footage licence — a stock-music subscription does not
      automatically cover a stock-footage subscription's audio, and vice versa
- [ ] CapCut's **built-in library audio is not cleared for commercial use** even on Pro.
      For any paid or branded delivery, license the track separately.
- [ ] If you use any AI-generated or AI-enhanced clip, check whether the platform requires
      disclosure, and whether your client's policy permits it

**Per project**
- [ ] A single document listing every clip: shot ID → library → clip URL → licence type →
      invoice → release status. This is what a client's legal team will ask for, and
      assembling it after delivery takes five times as long.
- [ ] If any editorial-only clip survived into the cut, the delivery is **non-commercial only**
      and that limitation is stated in writing to the client.

**Cultural note.** Intore was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible
Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2024. It is living heritage, not generic "tribal dance"
b-roll. Name the dance correctly in metadata and credits, avoid the "tribal/primitive"
vocabulary that dominates stock keywords when you write your own descriptions, and if the
film carries any commercial benefit, licensing from a Rwandan troupe is both the better
footage and the better relationship.

## 9. Zero-budget path

If there is no money at all, the film is still makeable — smaller, but honest:

1. **Pexels, Pixabay, Mixkit, Videvo** for landscape, water, hills, recreation, fire, portraits.
2. **UNESCO's Intore B-roll** — request permission in writing; cultural and educational
   requests are sometimes granted free or cheap.
3. **A Rwandan troupe's own YouTube/Vimeo channel** — contact them. A credit-and-link
   arrangement for a non-commercial film is a reasonable ask and is often accepted.
4. Cut Act 4A from 3 shots to 1, and Act 4B from 16 shots to 9. Use the freeze-and-release
   move instead of slow motion throughout — it costs nothing and it looks intentional.
5. Drop to 1080p delivery and 30 fps, and re-spec the ramps per §5.
6. Say plainly in the credits where every clip came from. It costs nothing and it is the
   difference between a scrappy homage and something that looks like it stole.

## 10. Suggested sourcing order

1. **Decide Option 1 / 2 / 3 (§2) and write it down.** Everything else depends on it.
2. **Search Act 4 first**, not last. It is the highest-risk act and the answer determines
   whether the film is the film you designed.
3. Contact Rwandan troupes and contributors in parallel with the library search — those
   conversations take days, not minutes.
4. Buy the five hero shots (§4) once you know Act 4 is solvable.
5. Buy Acts 1, 5 and 6 from one subscription collection each, so they match internally.
6. Source Act 3 last and cheapest — generic recreation footage is interchangeable, and by
   then you will know exactly how many seconds you actually need.
7. Fill Tier-D shots with builds (crop-ins, masks, freeze frames) once everything else is on
   the timeline and you can see what the holes really look like.
