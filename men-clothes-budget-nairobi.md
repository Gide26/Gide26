## 6. Customs — this is the big risk with new clothes

Uganda's allowance is **$2,000 for personal-use goods** if you've been out of the country 24h+. **It does not cover goods for resale.** New clothing in commercial quantity is the *most* likely thing to be flagged — unlike mitumba, there's no ambiguity about it being stock.

Worst case on new clothing (non-EAC origin — China/Turkey/Dubai brands, which most Eastleigh stock is):

| | Tier B (KSh 27,500) | Tier C (KSh 39,500) |
|---|---|---|
| Import duty @ 35% | KSh 9,625 | KSh 13,825 |
| VAT 18% on (value + duty) | KSh 6,682 | KSh 9,599 |
| **Total** | **≈ KSh 16,300 (~$126)** | **≈ KSh 23,400 (~$180)** |

Add IDF/levies and it can reach ~$140–160 on Tier B.

**One legitimate saving:** if any supplier stocks **Kenyan-made** garments, ask for an **EAC Certificate of Origin**. EAC-origin goods enter Uganda at **0% duty** (VAT still applies). Most Eastleigh stock is imported, so this usually won't apply — but Kenyan garment manufacturers do exist and it's worth asking.

**Practical rule:** the $2,000 allowance won't save you if the officer decides 45 new pieces is commercial. Keep every receipt, declare honestly, and budget for the duty rather than hoping it's waived.
